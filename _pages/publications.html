My research explores why some faults creep while others don't, and develops a set of criteria for positively identifying fault creep in the landscape and at the outcrop scale. The instrumental record of creep spans only about 60–70 years, while earthquake cycles play out over hundreds to thousands of years. By recognising evidence of creep in the geomorphic and geologic record, we can extend creep records far beyond the instrumental era and better understand the role creep plays in the earthquake cycle.

My earlier work at the University of Auckland investigated the Mangatangi Fault in the Hunua Range and Auckland's historic seismicity.

See also my research posters and talks.
